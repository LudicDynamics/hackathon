# AIRP aesthetic performance and attention

## Use this skill when

Use it when an experience needs visual, sonic, or motion direction. Read [the shared vocabulary](../_shared/shared-vocabulary.md) and ask `airp-causality-fairness-growth` which fact has landed. `airp-natural-flow-direct-manipulation` owns operation and recovery; `airp-human-language-information-hierarchy` owns readable status. An effect may amplify a fact but can never be the fact's only carrier.

## The product answer

Immersion is the player's continuing sense of **where they are, why they act, what responded, and how to continue**. Effects are optional expression: they should focus attention, carry emotion, and preserve spatial continuity without making the game depend on spectacle.

## Decide in this order

1. **Name the player purpose and settled fact.** What should the player notice, feel, or understand? If the fact is not settled, remove the performance or show only honest processing.
2. **Choose the smallest sensory treatment.** Select material, typography, colour, sound, motion, or transition by role. Do not add every medium by default. `[heuristic]` A primary focus is a starting hypothesis, not a universal one; comparison scenes may need multiple readable objects.
3. **Preserve spatial and attention continuity.** State the source, target, entry, exit, and final state. A character focus should still feel anchored to the canvas; a layer transition should preserve the player's sense of place.
4. **Start from the current visual language.** Use the v2 baseline—warm paper, ink, rounded surfaces, low-saturation rust/sage/blue, soft shadows, restrained motion—as a vocabulary, not a rigid theme. World material can vary when it preserves the product's information hierarchy and interaction grammar.
5. **Budget and delete.** Record focus count, moving/sound layers, duration, repetition, screen occupation, accessibility impact, performance cost, and maintenance cost. Keep only the layer that buys attention, emotion, spatial understanding, or action feedback.
6. **Define degradation.** With effects off, audio muted, media missing, a narrow screen, backgrounded tab, or low performance, retain text, entity state, spatial result, and durable fact. Do not use a failed image to erase a successful action.
7. **Review the last frame and revisit.** A reader should identify the important object and the settled result, then return later without a changed fact or forced replay. If the effect is the only reason the action feels complete, send the proposal back to `airp-causality-fairness-growth`, `airp-natural-flow-direct-manipulation`, or `airp-human-language-information-hierarchy`.

## Hard stop conditions

Delete or defer a performance when it precedes a causal decision, is the only state signal, obscures readable content, blocks operation with effects disabled, changes a fact on replay, or makes a media failure block the text path. “More immersive” is not evidence.

## Decision output

Return: player purpose; settled fact; sensory role; focus and continuity; Keep/Delete/Hide decision; accessibility/performance/degradation path; revisit result; evidence level and unknowns. Hand factual ordering to `airp-causality-fairness-growth`, operation to `airp-natural-flow-direct-manipulation`, wording to `airp-human-language-information-hierarchy`, and total complexity to `airp-simplicity-and-generalization`.
