# AIRP decision governance

## Use this skill when

Use it for a consequential product decision, not for drafting a single local idea. Load one domain skill for the proposal itself, then use this skill to challenge its value, scope, evidence, reversibility, and synchronization.

Read [the evidence ladder](../_shared/evidence-and-decision-states.md) and [the shared vocabulary](../_shared/shared-vocabulary.md).

## The product answer

No proposal is “done” because it is registered, implemented, or successful once. A decision is ready only when the player result, hard gates, alternatives, evidence level, and reopen condition are explicit.

## Decide in this order

1. **State the outcome and hypothesis.** What will the player do, see, understand, or feel differently? What observation would prove the idea wrong?
2. **Name non-negotiables.** Include understandable intent, truthful stage, causal result, recovery, revisit consistency, and any domain-specific safety gate. Do not replace a gate with a softer adjective.
3. **Compare candidates, including “do nothing.”** For each option, record player value, complexity, failure, reversibility, content/operations cost, and what it prevents. “More features” is not a candidate argument.
4. **Design the smallest discriminating slice.** Include a player action, visible response, readable durable result, meaningful next choice, and at least one failure, interruption, or repeat comparison. A static mockup is design evidence, not runtime proof.
5. **Run domain coverage.** Check only the domains touched: play/sandbox, causality, simplicity/generalization, direct manipulation, human language, and aesthetic performance. For merged domains, ask both component questions. Do not load every skill by habit.
6. **Grade the claim.** Label it E0 intent, E1 design, E2 static, E3 live path, or E4 repeatability. Record version, world, path, observation, and what remains unproved. A screenshot, HTTP success, model sentence, or log entry alone cannot be E3.
7. **Close the decision.** Choose `keep`, `revise`, `defer`, or `delete`. A defer needs an owner and reopen trigger; a keep needs its evidence boundary; a delete needs the counterfactual; a revise needs the missing condition.
8. **Synchronize truth.** Update the owning product/design source in the same change as the decision. If a conflict is unresolved, stop the promise and mark it `[unknown]`; never let a skill example silently become policy.

## Hard stop conditions

Do not call a proposal validated from a registration, one happy path, screenshot, static file, HTTP acceptance, or model reply. Do not close an unknown with “test later” unless it has an owner, evidence action, and trigger. Do not allow an unresolved fact, state, gesture, or cross-world boundary to hide inside a description or example.

## Decision output

Return: player outcome and hypothesis; non-negotiables; candidates including no-op; smallest slice; domains consulted; failure/revisit comparison; E-level and observation; keep/revise/defer/delete; unknown owner and reopen trigger; sources to synchronize.
